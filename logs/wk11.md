## Questions for Mario

1. When a company says "we want to use AI," how does your team figure out which problem is actually worth solving first, and what usually makes a pilot project fail to become something people use every day?
2. Since so many of us can now build small AI tools with an API and a few lines of code, what skills do you think will still make a Babson business student stand out in AI go-to-market roles over the next few years?
3. Your job sits between building AI products and selling them. As AI makes it faster and cheaper to build, how do you see building and selling changing, and will AI bring the people who build and the people who sell closer together?

## Something Mario said that I did not expect

Mario said that selling is really selling the client the opportunity to use a product in a way that benefits them, not just selling the product itself. I usually think of selling as convincing someone to buy a thing, so it surprised me that he framed it around the client's opportunity and benefit instead.

He also said that with AI, the new tech language is English, so we should not set barriers for ourselves. I used to think of tech as something only for people who can code, but if I can explain what I want clearly in plain English, I can build things too, which is what I've been doing with my Ask AI page and other projects this semester.

# OIM3690 2026 Fall Week 07

## What I built

- `discount.html` is the **Member Discount** exercise from class on 10/15: a cart total input, a member checkbox, and a `getDiscount` function that returns 20% off if the total is $100+ *and* you're a member, 10% off if either one is true on its own, and 0% otherwise. The page shows the discount amount and the final total after clicking "Calculate Discount."
- Other than that one in-class exercise, I barely touched `oim3690` this week — almost all of my time went into **Mini Project 2: Lesson Prep Planner**, which lives in its own repo ([vegalh/lesson-prep-planner](https://github.com/vegalh/lesson-prep-planner)) with its own GitHub Pages site (https://vegalh.github.io/lesson-prep-planner/). It's a planning tool for a Montessori/early-childhood classroom teacher: it stores a fixed catalog of practical-life activities, lets her schedule them onto dates, tracks which materials are ready for each scheduled activity, and shows at a glance how much total prep time each day needs.

I followed the same strict process I used for Mini Project 1: `PROPOSAL.md` (written by me, no AI) → `PRD.md` (data model, actions, calculated outputs, validation, page states) → several design-trial pages in `design-trials/` → `DESIGN.md` (the direction I actually picked) → `AGENTS.md` (rules for what the AI was and wasn't allowed to do while building) → the real tool (`index.html`, `styles.css`, `app.js`, `activities-data.js`).

What the finished tool does:

- Ships with 16 starter activities (food prep, care of self, care of the environment, fine motor, nature/sensory play) based on real classroom reference photos, each with an age range, duration, prep time, and materials list.
- Lets the teacher schedule any starter activity onto a date.
- Gives every scheduled entry its **own independent materials checklist** — scheduling "Watering Plants" twice makes two separate entries, each with its own ready/not-ready checkboxes, instead of sharing one.
- Calculates total prep minutes per day and shows a "Needs Prep"/"Ready" status badge for each scheduled activity.
- Lets her filter the upcoming-activities list by date, remove one entry, or reset the whole plan.
- Saves scheduled entries to `localStorage`, same pattern as my housing points tracker and to-do app, so the plan survives a page refresh.

## What gave me trouble

- Landing on one visual direction took five separate design trials, not one. The first (warm serif, card grid) and a cooler/denser alternative were both rejected before I asked to match the colors and component style of my other project, **rootmapmvp**.
- I wanted to reuse rootmapmvp's exact fonts (Nunito + Caveat), but that project loads them from Google Fonts over a CDN, and the rules I wrote for myself in `AGENTS.md` don't allow outside font CDNs for this project. Had to fall back to a system font stack instead.
- The first version of the chosen design (Trial 5) used a cursive stand-in font for headings and had a layout bug where every material checkbox stretched full-width and didn't line up with its label anymore. I had to actually flag both problems out loud ("the cursive heading font is bad and the spacing is not working, go and check") before they got fixed — the fix (bold sans-serif headings instead of cursive, and scoping the `width: 100%` rule to just text/date inputs instead of all inputs) is documented in `DESIGN.md` now as a pitfall to avoid.
- The trial pages all had a decorative "Practical Life" category tag on each card. I almost kept it, but caught that it wasn't actually part of the data model I'd written down in `PRD.md`, so I left it out instead of inventing a field that was never planned for.
- Not everything needed a fix, though — the toggle-material, remove-entry, filter-by-date, and reset interactions worked exactly as given on the first try. I still tested every one of them live in the browser before accepting the code (checking a material flipped the badge, the date filter narrowed the list without touching saved data, Reset cleared everything but left the activity dropdown fully populated) instead of just assuming it was fine because it "looked right."

This was the first mini project where I actually had a written PRD before any code existed, and having it written down made it a lot more obvious — to both me and the AI — when a suggestion (like the category tag) didn't actually belong.
